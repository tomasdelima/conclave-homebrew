How Maliced Lands Monsters Work

Every monster in this collection was made by people feeling the same thing together for long enough. Fear, grief, rage, envy, longing, lust, joy, shame, and pride all do it, and none of them is special. Once made, a monster is real and independent: the feeling can fade and the monster stays. There is no ritual of calm and no reconciliation that unmakes one. They have to be destroyed by hand.

They want territory and subjects, not extermination. A monster keeps its people alive, inside its domain, and feeling, because that is the ground it grows in.

Clan

Monsters bred by the same landscape fight the same way, and each clan carries one shared trait that says how. A stat block's clan trait is always the first trait listed.

  • Tidebound (coast and sea). Strong in the water, badly weakened on dry ground.
  • Underleaf (deep forest). Fights from cover that can be burned or cleared.
  • Deepsign (mountains and mines). Reads the rock, and is blunted by an uncovered open flame.
  • Crowded Ground (walled cities). Strong against people standing close together, weak against people who spread out.
  • Anchored (urban haunts). Carries a physical object that is part of its body, with its own AC and Hit Points, that can be broken or covered.

Fused monsters at the top of the range carry more than one clan's rules at once, in visibly separate organs.

Trigger

The emotion that made a monster decides whom it preys on. Each monster says so in plain words in its own traits; there is no table to look up.

  • Fear-born things key on Frightened creatures.
  • Grief-born things key on creatures below half their Hit Points and creatures near a corpse.
  • Rage-born things key on whoever has damaged them, and grow worse the more different people join in.
  • Envy-born things key on whoever has not been hurt yet.
  • Longing-born things key on creatures with no conscious ally within 30 feet.
  • Lust-born things key on Charmed creatures.
  • Joy-born things key on Prone creatures that can hear them.
  • Shame-born things key on Prone creatures with a witness nearby.
  • Pride-born things key on creatures that have just failed a saving throw against them, and are powerless against anyone who kneels.

Counter

Every monster here can be switched off, at least partly, by something the players can discover: a rule from its origin, a condition to end, an object to break, a formation to hold. The counter is printed in the stat block and repeated in the lore entry. Refusing a monster what feeds it is meant to be a real tactical resource, and the published Challenge Rating assumes the party finds the counter for roughly half the fight.

A monster's counter never requires a character to be immune to anything. If your players work out the rule, let it work.

At the table

This material is written for a mature table and its artwork is explicit. Nothing in any stat block requires a player to disclose a real secret, describe a real fear, reveal a character's private inventory, or roleplay a confession. Where a monster's origin touches those subjects, the mechanics are deliberately kept to conditions, damage, and position.

Challenge Ratings in this collection are design targets. They have not been verified at a table.

A forearm and hand, severed cleanly above the elbow, grey and swollen to twice its proper size and bound in knotted kelp. There is no wound where it ends, only a cuff of salt-white shell. It lies under ankle-deep surf, fingers spread, and does not move until something warm steps over it.

Domain and habits

A tidewrack hand owns about forty feet of one beach. It does not hunt; it waits at the place where people cross from dry stone into the water, which on most shores is the same four or five flat rocks everyone has used for a century. It wants the crossing kept frightening, and it is content to take one ankle a season.

Clan: Coast and sea. Born of fear. It was made by people feeling the same thing together for long enough, and it is now independent of them. Nobody's change of heart will unmake it; it has to be destroyed by hand.

Signs

Children stop using a particular stepping stone and cannot explain why. Crab pots set at that spot come up with the lines wound tight anticlockwise.

What is known

What the people of its domain will tell you, if the Hush has not got to them first:

  • The shallows at the old slipway pull harder than they should, even at slack water.
  • A crewman of the Marion drowned in two feet of water with his boots still laced.
  • There is a whole drowned man under the sand there, and only his hand has worked loose.
  • Salt thrown on the water keeps it down.

The first two are true. There is no drowned man, and salt does nothing.

The counter

It is only dangerous in the water. A creature standing on dry rock is almost safe from it, and the hand that follows onto stone is slow, clumsy, and easy to break.

Running it

Open with the grip, not the sight: have a character feel fingers close on an ankle before anyone sees what has hold of them. It is a first-session monster, and its job is to teach the table that these things obey rules. Let the party work out within two rounds that the beach is safe and the water is not.

Description

A squat, headless animal of root and bark, about the size of a dog. Its ribcage is a cage of bark staves, and inside it, turned sideways, is a single human ear. Clusters of closed seed-eyes run down its spine and never open. It moves on thin root toes and it is always, visibly, listening.

Domain and habits

A rootlisten owns a household: one cottage, one clearing, one family's private conversations. It does not want the family dead. It wants them careful, and a household that has learned to whisper indoors and say nothing on the path is a household it can live beside for years.

Clan: Deep forest. Born of fear. It was made by people feeling the same thing together for long enough, and it is now independent of them. Nobody's change of heart will unmake it; it has to be destroyed by hand.

Signs

Trail marks loop back on themselves; a Hush decree forbids singing the lost-road ballad.

What is known

What the people of its domain will tell you, if the Hush has not got to them first:

  • Say anything aloud under those trees and something moves toward you.
  • It repeats what you said back in your own voice before it comes.
  • It cannot hear you if you stay still.
  • Burning the undergrowth drives it out.

The first, second and fourth are true. Standing still does not help; silence does.

The counter

It works entirely on speech. It cannot frighten anyone who has not spoken aloud or cast with a verbal component since its last turn, and its reaction only triggers on sound. Fire also strips the cover it fights from.

Running it

Use it to establish the forest clan's rule at the lowest possible stakes. It is a nuisance, not a threat, and the correct outcome is that the party learns to shut up in this forest before they meet something that matters. If a caster insists on verbal components, let it work — then let them see what it costs them.

Description

A knee-high tangle of soaked netting folded around a circular, lipless mouth of small hooked teeth. Fishing hooks serve it as jointed feet. Two black pearls sit where eyes would be, and the mesh is still caught with the silver scales of an ordinary catch. Hauled aboard, it lies exactly as dead gear lies.

Domain and habits

A netmouth owns a boat rather than a stretch of water, and travels with it. It lives in the pile of gear at the stern, eats what it can take from a crew of four or five, and is careful never to take so many that the boat stops going out. A harbor that has one usually has several, each on its own hull.

Clan: Coast and sea. Born of fear. It was made by people feeling the same thing together for long enough, and it is now independent of them. Nobody's change of heart will unmake it; it has to be destroyed by hand.

Signs

Empty nets turn toward shore before it appears, and the Hush has quietly removed names from the harbor rolls.

What is known

What the people of its domain will tell you, if the Hush has not got to them first:

  • Gear left on deck overnight is found moved, always closer to where the crew sleep.
  • It can only be killed by burning the whole net, which means burning the boat.
  • It plays dead until something warm touches it; a boat hook is safe, a bare hand is not.
  • It came up in a haul off the Ninestones in the famine year.

The first and third are true. Fire hurts it badly but does not require the boat; the Ninestones story is told about every netmouth on the coast.

The counter

Fire is its real weakness: any burn strips the armor it takes from a frightened crew for a round. It also cannot frighten anyone who has not touched the net, so a crew that works the gear with gaffs and hooks is far safer than one that works it by hand.

Running it

Describe the heap of net twice before it moves, once when the party boards and once when they search. The monster is a punishment for a careless action, so make the careless action available and let someone take it. On a small deck its ability to share a space makes it genuinely frightening at low level.

Description

A squat figure the size of a child, cast in candle tallow over a frame of pit props, with a lamp-flame burning somewhere inside its chest where the wax has gone translucent. Its knuckles are iron. It knocks on the rock the way a trapped man knocks, in threes, and then it waits the way a trapped man waits.

Domain and habits

A tallow knocker owns one district of one mine: a few hundred yards of roadway and the men who work it. It wants the knocking believed in. A crew that has learned to stop and listen every time is a crew working slowly and fearfully, which is exactly the condition it lives on, and it will go years without killing anyone.

Clan: Mountains and mines. Born of fear. It was made by people feeling the same thing together for long enough, and it is now independent of them. Nobody's change of heart will unmake it; it has to be destroyed by hand.

Signs

Ventilation doors are found sealed that nobody sealed; the lodge's accident book has pages gummed together.

What is known

What the people of its domain will tell you, if the Hush has not got to them first:

  • Three knocks means the door has gone and you have about a minute.
  • It will not come near a man with an open lamp.
  • It is the last of the Hallow Drift crew, still knocking.
  • Safety lamps are no protection; the flame has to be bare.

The second and fourth are true. The knocking means nothing in particular, and no crew it belongs to ever existed.

The counter

An uncovered open flame within thirty feet takes away its attack accuracy and its bonus action outright. A covered safety lamp does nothing — which is a cruel joke, since safety lamps are what miners actually carry.

Running it

The lamp rule is the lesson of the whole mines clan, so teach it here at CR 1. Put the party underground with safety lamps and let them work out that they need to open one, and that opening one in a gassy roadway is its own decision. It fights from the dark and retreats into it.

Description

A broad, swollen figure in the shape of an adult relative, blue-grey and crusted with salt, its throat lengthened by a hand's width so the voice carries. Weed and abandoned netting hang from its shoulders like a coat. It carries nothing and threatens nothing. It stands at the tide line and says names.

Domain and habits

A drowned caller owns a parish of perhaps two hundred people and intends to keep every one of them. It works at dusk at the edge of the water, calling the names of the living in the voices of their dead, and it only ever takes those who answer. A household that has learned not to answer can live inside its territory for three generations, which is exactly what the caller wants.

Clan: Coast and sea. Born of fear. It was made by people feeling the same thing together for long enough, and it is now independent of them. Nobody's change of heart will unmake it; it has to be destroyed by hand.

Signs

Someone's drowned relative is heard calling from the water at dusk; the parish register has pages cut out where the drowned should be.

What is known

What the people of its domain will tell you, if the Hush has not got to them first:

  • It calls in the voice of someone you have lost, and it gets the voice exactly right.
  • If you answer it even once, it can always find you afterward.
  • It cannot come above the tide line.
  • Speaking its own name back to it destroys it.

The first two are true. It can leave the water, though it is much weaker on dry ground, and nothing is destroyed by naming.

The counter

It cannot frighten anyone who has stayed silent. A party that works the shore without speaking aloud — no shouted orders, no verbal spellcasting — strips it of its central ability entirely, and that constraint is worth the whole encounter.

Running it

This is the monster that teaches a table to read a rule. Run it twice if you can: once as an event the party only witnesses, from a window, where a neighbor answers and is taken; and once as a fight, after they have worked out what answering costs. The silence constraint makes casters re-plan, which is the point.

Description

A tall figure built out of driftwood and salt crust, hollow through the chest where a body should be, arms permanently open in the shape of an embrace that has nothing in it. It always faces the water. Wood grain runs the wrong way across its face, which has no features except the direction it is turned.

Domain and habits

A shorewaiter owns the waiting itself. Its territory is any shore where people still come to watch for ships, and the people it wants are the ones who keep coming. It is not hostile to strangers and does not hunt the parish. It becomes dangerous only when someone tries to take a waiting person away from the water, or when a rival monster comes for the same widows.

Clan: Coast and sea. Born of longing. It was made by people feeling the same thing together for long enough, and it is now independent of them. Nobody's change of heart will unmake it; it has to be destroyed by hand.

Signs

Driftwood stacks itself into standing figures along the high-water mark; the Hush has twice relocated the village and twice it has walked back.

What is known

What the people of its domain will tell you, if the Hush has not got to them first:

  • It has never hurt anyone from this village.
  • It fought something in the water two winters ago and the sea boiled for an hour.
  • If you take a widow off that beach it will follow you inland.
  • It is the ship's figurehead, come back without the ship.

The first three are true. It has no connection to any figurehead, though every coastal village tells that version.

The counter

It does not attack a party that leaves its waiting people alone, and it will fight other coast monsters for them. Beyond that, it preys on whoever is isolated: nobody who keeps an ally within thirty feet is a target for its reaching.

Running it

Offer it as an alliance before offering it as a fight. The cleanest use is a three-way encounter on a beach: the party, a drowned caller that wants the same widows, and the shorewaiter, which will spend itself on the caller if the party stands aside. If the party does attack it, punish spreading out — it pulls one character away and works on them alone.

A high funeral chair of black oak, worn pale on the arms, that walks. A place is laid at it — cloth, cup, plate — and the place is never filled. What sits in the chair is only visible as an absence with weight, and the chair carries it from house to house.

Domain and habits

A last seat owns a parish's funeral tables. It attends. It takes the empty place at every wake in its district and it stays until the household has stopped setting one, at which point it comes for them. Its subjects keep mourning on schedule because they have learned what happens when they stop.

Clan: Urban haunts. Born of grief. It was made by people feeling the same thing together for long enough, and it is now independent of them. Nobody's change of heart will unmake it; it has to be destroyed by hand.

Signs

A place is laid every night for someone long dead and the household cannot say who started it; licensed mourning hours end early by decree.

What is known

What the people of its domain will tell you, if the Hush has not got to them first:

  • It sits down at wakes. If you are hosting, you lay the extra place.
  • It gets stronger while there are wounded near the dead.
  • Throw a cloth over the chair and it is blind and harmless.
  • It is the first person this parish buried.

The first three are true. It was never a person.

The counter

The chair is the organ, and it can be covered. A creature within five feet can spend an action to throw an opaque cloth over it — or simply destroy it, AC 12 and 15 Hit Points — and the seat stops healing from the wounded entirely. It can spend its own action to uncover it again.

Running it

This is the clan's teaching encounter: the furniture is the monster, and the party should work that out by hitting the chair by accident. A funeral in progress makes the best arena, because the wounded and the dead are already in the room. Its vigil attack needs a line of sight to the chair, so covering it does double duty.

Legendary Action Uses: 3. Immediately after another creature's turn, the country can expend a use to take one of the following actions. It regains all expended uses at the start of each of its turns.

Border Crossing

The country moves up to half its Speed without provoking Opportunity Attacks. The ground it leaves is Difficult Terrain for creatures other than it until the start of its next turn.

Province Bite (Costs 2 Uses)

The country makes one Province Bite attack.

The Border Moves (Costs 2 Uses)

Strength Saving Throw: DC 26, each creature in a 30-foot Emanation originating from the country. Failure: The target is pulled up to 20 feet toward the country and has the Prone condition.

Lair Actions

On Initiative Count 20 (losing Initiative ties), the country takes a Lair Action to cause one of the following effects; it can't use the same effect two rounds in a row:

  • The border moves. Each creature within 120 feet of the country makes a DC 26 Strength saving throw or is pulled 20 feet toward it and has the Prone condition.
  • Five mouths speak at once and disagree. Each creature within 90 feet of the country makes a DC 26 Wisdom saving throw, taking 24 (7d6) Psychic damage on a failed save and half as much on a success.
  • The ground remembers another country. A 60-foot-radius area the country can see within 120 feet becomes water, forest floor, worked stone, or city flagstone, the country's choice, until the end of its next turn. That area is Difficult Terrain for creatures other than the country.

Regional Effects

The country within 6 miles of the country's borders is altered in the following ways:

  • No two people within 6 miles agree on which country they are standing in, and all of them are correct.
  • Every feeling in the region is held in common. A private grief becomes a parish's grief inside a day.
  • The Hush has abandoned this country. Its records end mid-sentence, and its officers are among the dead here.

If the country dies, these effects end immediately, and the five countries it ate stay eaten.
