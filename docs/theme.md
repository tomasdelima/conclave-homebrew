# The Unfinished Dawn setting proposal

This is a provisional world theme for an original bestiary, spells, and magic items using the revised fifth edition rules. The first three creatures test the same ecology at CR 1/8, 5, and 13. Names, tone, and cosmology can change together before the collection expands.

## The broken celestial engine

Morning once passed through a vast celestial engine called the Meridian. When it fractured, pieces fell into the world and retained different properties of dawn: light, warmth, direction, and awakening. Ordinary day and night continue, but near the wreckage, local mornings can arrive late, linger underground, or illuminate a room without warming it.

The cause of the fracture remains open. It could have been sabotage, a failed repair, or a deliberate attempt to free the world from something the engine controlled. No answer needs to be canon yet.

## A living world around the wreckage

People build towns around useful fragments. Glass farmers cultivate warm lenses beneath winter fields. Bell keepers mark safe crossings when the local sunrise is out of step with the sky. Salvagers recover brass from fallen observatories, sometimes waking a custodian that considers the town an unauthorized repair.

The fragments are a resource with ecological consequences. Gleaners move them, prowlers hunt around them, and kilns reclaim them. Creatures have diets, habitats, and reasons to avoid a fight. Strange doesn't automatically mean hostile.

## Shared appearance

Use charcoal fur or stone, pale ivory ceramic, weathered brass, translucent amber glass, and slate-blue dusk. Living creatures grow these materials into their bodies; custodians were built from them. Hairline golden cracks and incomplete circles connect the designs without giving every creature the same silhouette.

Full illustrations show the whole subject in its habitat. Tokens use a close portrait inside a raised brass and copper ring, with transparent space outside the circle. Keep the face or identifying core legible on a battle map. Retain the same anatomy, colors, and distinguishing features in both versions.

## How powers belong to the world

Prefer one concrete relationship to a dawn fragment over a list of unrelated abilities. Light may attract a scavenger, provide a predator's movement route, or reveal a custodian's survey. Give players observable clues and a practical response. Don't make the whole bestiary resistant to Radiant damage.

Future spells can redirect light, store warmth, or awaken damaged mechanisms. Future items should have an identifiable maker or material and a use in this world. Their names, mechanics, full artwork, and matching tokens should all grow from the same source.

## The first three creatures

| Creature | CR target | Role | Connection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glimmer Gleaner | 1/8 | Scavenger and nuisance | Spreads fragments through its nests |
| Vesperglass Prowler | 5 | Territorial predator | Hunts glowing prey along cached lenses |
| Kiln of the Ninth Dawn | 13 | Custodian and negotiable boss | Reclaims fragments to repair the Meridian |

A small adventure follows a stolen lighthouse lens: relocate gleaners, survive the prowler following their trail, then bargain with the kiln that claims the lens. Each creature can resolve a different kind of encounter.

## Expansion after theme review

The eventual bestiary spans CR 1/8 through CR 30. Plan families across nuisance creatures, wilderness threats, regional powers, and world-scale custodians, with varied creature types and combat roles. These first three aren't evidence that every future creature needs six limbs, ceramic armor, or a light attack.

Before expansion, settle how hopeful or frightening the setting should be, whether the Meridian is divine or mortal craft, and how common useful fragments are. Spells and items remain empty in this milestone so those decisions can guide them.
